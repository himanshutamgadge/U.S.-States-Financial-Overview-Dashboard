# 📊 U.S. States Financial Overview Dashboard

An interactive Excel dashboard to visualize and analyze the financial health of U.S. states across multiple years.

---

## 🧾 Dataset

- **Time Period:** 1992–2019
- **Key Columns:**
  - Total Revenue
  - Total Expenditure
  - Surplus/Deficit
  - Total Debt
  - Detailed Expenditure Categories (Health, Education, Welfare, etc.)
  - State, Year

---

## 📌 Features

- Built entirely in **Excel** using:
  - **Power Pivot**
  - **Slicers & Timeline Filters**
  - **DAX Measures**
- **KPIs displayed:**
  - Total Revenue
  - Total Expenditure
  - Surplus/Deficit
  - Total Debt
  - Debt-to-Revenue Ratio
- **Charts included:**
  - 📈 Revenue vs Expenditure (Line Chart)
  - 📊 Debt-to-Revenue Ratio (Bar Chart)
  - 📦 Expenditure Breakdown (Stacked Column)
  - 🗺️ Revenue Map by State (Filled Map)
  - 🏆 Top 10 and Bottom 10 States by Debt-to-Revenue Ratio (Scatter Plots)

---

## 📈 Analysis Summary

- Most states maintain a **surplus**, indicating good fiscal management.
- **California** stands out with high total revenue and stable expenditure, maintaining a healthy debt ratio.
- States like **Massachusetts, New York, and Connecticut** exhibit higher debt-to-revenue ratios, indicating a greater fiscal burden.
- **Expenditure patterns** reveal major spending on **Health, Education, and Welfare** across all states.
- Debt-to-revenue ratios and trends vary significantly, indicating diverse fiscal strategies.

---

## ✅ Conclusion

- U.S. states overall show **strong financial health**.
- While revenue has increased steadily over the years, some states carry a **higher debt burden** relative to income.
- Spending is consistently focused on essential services, though its distribution varies.

---

## 💡 Recommendations

- **High-debt states** should evaluate borrowing and spending practices.
- Use **benchmarking** against fiscally strong states to set performance goals.
- Leverage **data dashboards** like this for transparent public finance tracking.
- Focus on maintaining a **surplus** while optimizing capital outlay.
